Chapter 9

Joins

Normalization (chapter 8) spreads one fact across several tables. A join puts them back together for a query: it combines rows of two tables that are related through a common column. Every join below is worked on the same two tables, so you can compare the results side by side.

9.1Why Joins and Types of Joins (Two Friends Invited to Dinner)

🎓 Lecture topic (from outline) The data you want often sits in two tables. "Which students can take the exam in their own city?" needs Student (who lives where) and Centre (which city has a centre). A join answers it in one query.

Analogy: two friends invited to dinner

Think of each table as one friend's guest list, and the common column as the thing that pairs guests up. An inner join seats only the pairs where both sides turn up. An outer join also seats the guests who came without a partner, with an empty chair (NULL) beside them.

Types of joins

  • Cross product (×): every row paired with every row.
  • Inner joins, which keep only matching rows: theta join (any condition), equi join (condition uses =), natural join (⋈, equality on all same-named columns).
  • Self join: a table joined with itself.
  • Outer joins, which also keep non-matching rows: left (⟕), right (⟖), full (⟗).

9.2Cross (Cartesian) Product

🎓 Lecture topic (from outline) Student × Centre pairs every Student row with every Centre row, with no condition at all.
  • Rows = 3 × 3 = 9. Columns = 3 + 2 = 5.
  • Most of these rows are meaningless: (Asha, Delhi, E3, Mumbai) pairs a Delhi student with a Mumbai centre.
  • Every other join is a cross product followed by a filter. That's why the cross product is the base operator.
|R × S| = |R| × |S| rows · degree(R × S) = degree(R) + degree(S)
➕ Added for exam
  • In SQL: SELECT * FROM Student CROSS JOIN Centre; or simply FROM Student, Centre with no WHERE.
  • If either table is empty, the cross product is empty.
  • Forgetting the join condition in a WHERE clause silently turns a join into a cross product, a classic bug.

9.3Natural Join and Equi Join (Exam in the Home City)

🎓 Lecture topic (from outline) "Which students can write the exam in their home city?" Keep only the cross-product rows where Student.City = Centre.City.

Equi join: Student ⋈Student.City = Centre.City Centre

A join whose condition uses only =. Both City columns stay in the result.

Natural join: Student ⋈ Centre

No condition is written. The join automatically matches on all columns with the same name (here, City) and keeps that column once.

1AshaDelhiE1

🎓 Lecture topic (from outline) Neha (Jaipur) has no centre, and centre E3 (Mumbai) has no student, so neither appears. Both are inner joins.
Natural join = cross product → keep rows where same-named columns are equal → drop the duplicate column
⚠️ Exam trap
  • If the two tables share no column name, a natural join gives the cross product.
  • If they share a column name by accident (say both have a "Name" column meaning different things), the natural join matches on it too and gives wrong results. That's why natural join is risky in real SQL.
  • Equi join keeps both copies of the join column; natural join keeps one.

9.4Self Join (Students in More Than One Subject)

🎓 Lecture topic (from outline) A self join joins a table with itself, using two aliases so each copy can be named. It's how you compare rows within one table.

Example: which students are enrolled in more than one subject?

🎓 Lecture topic (from outline) Take two copies, E1 and E2, of Enrol. Look for pairs of rows with the same Roll but a different Subject:
SELECT DISTINCT E1.Roll FROM Enrol E1, Enrol E2 WHERE E1.Roll = E2.Roll AND E1.Subject <> E2.Subject;
Roll 1 pairs (DBMS, OS) and Roll 3 pairs (CN, OS); Roll 2 has only one row, so it can never find a partner. Answer: 1, 3.
⚠️ Exam trap Without E1.Subject <> E2.Subject, every row matches itself and every student qualifies. Without DISTINCT, Roll 1 appears twice (DBMS–OS and OS–DBMS).

9.5Left, Right and Full Outer Join

🎓 Lecture topic (from outline) An inner join drops rows that have no match. An outer join keeps them and fills the missing side with NULL.
  • Left outer (Student ⟕ Centre): all Student rows; Neha gets NULL for CentreID.
  • Right outer (Student ⟖ Centre): all Centre rows; E3 gets NULL for Roll and Name.
  • Full outer (Student ⟗ Centre): all rows of both.

Full outer join = left outer join ∪ right outer join
➕ Added for exam
  • SQL: Student LEFT JOIN Centre ON Student.City = Centre.City; likewise RIGHT JOIN and FULL OUTER JOIN. MySQL has no FULL OUTER JOIN; it's written as a LEFT JOIN UNION a RIGHT JOIN.
  • A ⟕ B gives the same rows as B ⟖ A (only the column order differs).
  • Left outer join rows ≥ rows of the left table. Full outer join rows ≥ the larger of the two tables.
  • To find rows with no match (students with no centre), use a left join and then WHERE Centre.CentreID IS NULL.

9.6Theta, Semi and Anti Joins; Counting RowsADDED

➕ Added for exam
  • Theta join R ⋈θ S = σθ(R × S), where θ can use any of =, ≠, <, ≤, >, ≥. An equi join is a theta join that uses only =.
  • Semi join (R ⋉ S): the rows of R that have at least one match in S, with only R's columns. SQL: WHERE EXISTS / IN.
  • Anti join (R ▷ S): the rows of R with no match in S. SQL: WHERE NOT EXISTS / NOT IN.

🎯 IBPS exam alert Common: "R has 10 rows, S has 5 rows. Maximum rows in R ⋈ S?" → 50 (every row matches every row). "Rows in R × S?" → exactly 50. And for a natural join on a foreign key that references S's primary key, with no NULL foreign keys → exactly 10, one match per R row.

9.7Solved Exam QuestionsADDED

Q1R has 4 columns and 6 rows; S has 3 columns and 5 rows. Degree and cardinality of R × S?
Answer: degree 7, cardinality 30.
Q2The natural join of two relations with no common attributes is:
Answer: their Cartesian product.
Q3Which join keeps all rows of the left table, filling NULLs where there is no match?
Answer: left outer join.
Q4How do equi join and natural join differ?
Answer: Equi join uses an explicit = condition and keeps both copies of the join column; natural join matches all same-named columns automatically and keeps one copy.
Q5To list employees together with their managers' names from a single Employee(EID, Name, MgrID) table, you need a:
Answer: self join (E.MgrID = M.EID).
Q6A theta join whose condition uses only equality is called a(n):
Answer: equi join.
Q7Full outer join is equivalent to:
Answer: the union of the left outer join and the right outer join.

⚡ Rapid Revision

  • Join = combine related rows of two tables through a common column; needed because normalization splits data.
  • Cross product: every row × every row; rows m × n, columns add up. The base of all joins.
  • Equi join: condition with =, both join columns kept. Natural join: auto-match on same-named columns, kept once. No common column ⇒ cross product.
  • Exam in the home city: Student ⋈ Centre on City → Asha (E1), Ravi (E2); Neha and E3 drop out.
  • Self join: one table, two aliases. More than one subject: same Roll, different Subject, DISTINCT.
  • Outer joins keep unmatched rows with NULLs: left (all left rows), right (all right rows), full (both). Full = left ∪ right.
  • ➕ Theta join = σθ(R × S); equi join is the = case. Semi join = EXISTS; anti join = NOT EXISTS.
  • ➕ Inner join: 0 to m × n rows. Left outer ≥ m. Full outer ≥ max(m, n).
