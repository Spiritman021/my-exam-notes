1Loans and Advances

ऋण और अग्रिम – what a bank does with the deposits it collects.

🏦 BankingWhere deposit money goes

  • The money collected through deposits is used to give loans like personal, home, car, education and business loans.
  • Banks charge interest on these loans. The difference between interest earned and interest paid is a major source of profit.
➕ Added for exam
  • That difference is called the bank’s spread. Net interest income divided by earning assets is the Net Interest Margin (NIM).

🎯 Likely QTypes of Advancesअग्रिमों के प्रकार

  • Three types taught: Cash Credit (नकद साख), Overdraft (अधिविकर्ष) and Bill Discounted (बिल बट्टा).

2Cash Credit (CC)

नकद साख – a working-capital limit for businesses.

🏦 BankingCash Creditनकद साख

  • Cash Credit is an arrangement whereby the bank allows the borrower to withdraw amount up to a certain specified limit.
  • Interest is charged on the amount actually withdrawn (not on the whole limit).
  • Cash Credit is granted as per terms and conditions agreed with the customer.
  • It is a short-term loan approved by banks for businesses, financial institutions and companies to meet their working capital requirements.
  • CC is a source of short-term finance for businesses and companies.
  • Cash credits are also called working capital loans, as they fund the instant cash requirements of organisations or the purchase of current assets.
  • Cash credit is available for a shorter period of time and at a significantly lower interest rate than overdrafts.
➕ Added for exam
  • Hypothecation = the stock / inventory is the security, but it stays with the borrower (the bank holds only a charge on it).

3Overdraft (OD)

अधिविकर्ष – drawing more than you have in your account.

🏦 BankingOverdraftअधिविकर्ष

  • Overdraft is also a credit facility granted by a bank.
  • A customer who has a current account with the bank is allowed to withdraw more than the credit balance in the account.
  • Overdraft is an extension of credit to current and savings account holders in cases where the total balance of the account becomes zero.
➕ Added for exam
  • Example: PMJDYPMJDYPradhan Mantri Jan-Dhan YojanaLaunched 28 Aug 2014National Mission for Financial InclusionZero-balance account + RuPay debit card accounts carry an overdraft facility of up to ₹10,000.

4Bill Discounted (BD)

बिल बट्टा – getting the money for a bill before its due date.

🏦 BankingBill Discountingबिल बट्टा

  • Banks provide short-term finance by discounting bills: they pay the amount before the due date of the bills, after deducting a certain rate of discount.
  • The party gets the funds without waiting for the due date of the bills.
  • If any bill is dishonoured on the due date, the bank can recover the amount from the customer.

Process of Bill Discounting – 7 steps

  • 1 Supply – goods / services delivered.
  • 2 Invoice – raised by the seller.
  • 3 Acceptance – buyer acknowledges the invoice.
  • 4 Application – seller approaches a financial institution.
  • 5 Verification – the institution checks the buyer’s credit.
  • 6 Disbursement – funds given to the seller minus the discount.
  • 7 Repayment – the buyer pays the institution on the due date.
➕ Added for exam
  • For MSMEs, RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai runs online invoice-discounting platforms called TReDS (Trade Receivables Discounting System).

5Payments Bank

भुगतान बैंक – Indian Banking System, Part 2.

🎯 Likely QPayments Bank – what it can and cannot doभुगतान बैंक

  • Payments Bank can accept a Demand Deposit (CASACASACurrent Account + Savings Account depositsLow-cost funds for a bankCASA ratio = CASA ÷ total deposits).
  • It cannot accept a Term Deposit (written “RAFA” on the slide – i.e. fixed / recurring deposits).
  • It can accept deposits of up to ₹2 lakh per customer.
  • Payments Bank cannot give loans.
  • It provides a Debit Card (ATM card).
  • Minimum capital required to open this bank is ₹100 crore.
  • 25% of a Payments Bank’s branches must be in the unbanked rural area.
  • Foreign shareholding is permitted as per the rule for FDI in private banks.
➕ Added for exam
  • Recommended by the Nachiket Mor Committee; RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai issued the guidelines in November 2014.
  • The deposit limit was ₹1 lakh at the start and was raised to ₹2 lakh in 2021.
  • It cannot issue credit cards, and must keep at least 75% of its demand deposits in government securities / treasury bills (SLR securities).
⚠️ Exam trapPayments bank: debit card yes, credit card no; savings & current yes, FD/RD no; loans no.

6Small Finance Bank (SFB)

लघु वित्त बैंक – deposits and loans for small borrowers.

🎯 Likely QSmall Finance Bank – the rulesलघु वित्त बैंक

  • Eligibility: 10 years’ experience in banking.
  • Small banks offer deposits as well as provide loans.
  • Minimum capital to open this bank is ₹300 crore.
  • Foreign shareholding is permitted as per the rule for FDI in private banks.
  • 25% of an SFB’s branches must be in the unbanked rural area.
  • SFBs have to lend 60% of their credit to the Priority Sector.
➕ Added for exam
  • Also from RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai’s 2014 guidelines. At least 50% of an SFB’s loan portfolio must be loans of up to ₹25 lakh.
  • The first SFB to start was Capital Small Finance Bank (2016).
⚠️ Exam trapCapital: Payments Bank ₹100 crore, Small Finance Bank ₹300 crore (as taught). Older books may show ₹100 crore or ₹200 crore for SFBs, from earlier RBI rules. Use the class figure, but if an option says “₹200 crore”, remember it comes from the 2019 rules.

8Self-test

Answer out loud first, then click to check.

What are the three types of advances taught?

Cash Credit, Overdraft and Bill Discounted.

In cash credit, interest is charged on what amount?

Only on the amount actually withdrawn.

Cash credits are also called what, and why?

Working capital loans – they fund instant cash needs or the purchase of current assets.

Which is cheaper – cash credit or overdraft?

Cash credit (lower interest rate).

What security backs a cash credit?

Hypothecation of stocks and inventory.

Which facility can be used for any purpose – CC or OD?

Overdraft. Cash credit is for business purposes only.

Which facility needs a new account to be opened?

Cash credit. Overdraft is given on an existing account.

Tenure: CC vs OD?

CC – generally a minimum of 1 year; OD – shorter (month/quarter), maximum 1 year.

In bill discounting, what happens if the bill is dishonoured?

The bank recovers the amount from the customer.

In the bill-discounting process, who repays the institution on the due date?

The buyer (step 7).

Can a payments bank accept fixed deposits or give loans?

No to both. It accepts only demand deposits (CASA).

Maximum deposit per customer in a payments bank?

₹2 lakh.

Minimum capital for a payments bank and a small finance bank (as taught)?

₹100 crore and ₹300 crore.

What share of branches must payments banks and SFBs have in unbanked rural areas?

At least 25%.

What share of credit must an SFB give to the priority sector?

60%.

Eligibility to promote an SFB?

10 years’ experience in banking.

Name the six payments banks.

Airtel, India Post, FINO, Paytm, Jio and NSDL Payments Bank.

How many small finance banks are listed in the class? Name any four.

11 – e.g. AU, Capital, Equitas, ESAF, Suryoday, Ujjivan, Utkarsh, Slice, Jana, Shivalik, Unity.

⚡ Rapid Revision

  • Bank profit = interest earned on loans − interest paid on deposits (spread).
  • Advances: Cash Credit · Overdraft · Bill Discounted.
  • CC: business only, stock hypothecation, lower rate, new account, ≥ 1 year, interest on amount withdrawn.
  • OD: any purpose, existing CA/SA, higher rate, ≤ 1 year, based on credit history / FDs / policies.
  • Bill discounting: paid before due date minus discount; dishonour → recovered from customer. 7 steps: supply → invoice → acceptance → application → verification → disbursement → repayment.
  • Payments Bank: CASA only, ≤ ₹2 lakh, no loans, debit card yes, ₹100 crore capital, 25% unbanked rural branches, FDI as private banks. 6 banks: Airtel, India Post, FINO, Paytm, Jio, NSDL.
  • SFB: deposits + loans, ₹300 crore capital (as taught), 60% PSL, 25% unbanked rural branches, 10 years’ banking experience. 11 banks.
