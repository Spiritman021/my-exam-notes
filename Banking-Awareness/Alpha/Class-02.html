1No-Frills Account → BSBD Account

The bank account built for financial inclusion (continues from Class 1).

🏦 BankingNo-Frills Accountनो-फ्रिल्स खाता

  • A no-frills account in India is a basic savings account designed for financial inclusion.
  • It is an account with zero or very low minimum balance.
  • Introduced by the Reserve Bank of India (RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai) in 2005.
  • It targets low-income and underserved groups.

🎯 Likely Q2012: renamed Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account (BSBDA)

  • In 2012, RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai renamed “no-frills accounts” to “Basic Savings Bank Deposit Accounts” (BSBDA) to improve financial inclusion.
⚠️ Exam trapTwo years to keep apart: no-frills account introduced 2005; renamed BSBDA in 2012.

RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai Kehta Hai – the BSBD awareness campaign

  • RBI Kehta Hai is a public awareness initiative of RBI.
  • Its BSBD message: “Don’t want to keep minimum balance in your account? Open BSBD Account.”
  • The ad “Banking becomes easy” says: open a BSBD account, no minimum balance required, easy to open with just Aadhaar Card and PAN Card or Form No. 60.
  • The ad features cricketers KL Rahul and Umesh Yadav (both captioned “Indian Cricketer and RBI Employee”).
➕ Added for exam
  • Form 60Form 60Declaration filed by a person who does not have a PANUsed for financial transactions instead of PAN is the declaration a person gives when they do not have a PAN.

🎯 Likely QBSBD Account – features (RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai Kehta Hai notice)

  • Can be opened by any individual, irrespective of his/her age or income.
  • Can be opened without any initial deposit. No need to maintain a minimum balance.
  • A regular savings bank account can be converted into a BSBD account at the request of the customer.
  • Basic facilities like an ATM-cum-Debit Card are given free of charge.
  • There is no limit on the number of deposits.
  • Holders get a maximum of four withdrawals free of charge in a month, counting ATM withdrawals, RTGS / NEFT / clearing / internet debits, standing instructions, EMIs, etc.
  • BSBD holders cannot have a regular savings bank account in the same bank.

🎯 Likely QBSBD Account – rules and limits

  • The holder of a BSBD account is not eligible to open any other savings bank account in the same bank.
  • If the customer already has a savings account in that bank, it must be closed within 30 days from the date of opening the BSBD account.
  • Total credits in such accounts should not exceed ₹1 lakh in a year.
  • The maximum balance should not exceed ₹50,000 at any time.
  • Total debits by way of cash withdrawals and transfers will not exceed ₹10,000 in a month.

2Banking in India – what is a Bank?

Indian Banking System, Part 1.

🏦 BankingDefinition of Bankबैंक

  • A bank is a financial institution which accepts deposits and provides loans & advances and other financial services.
  • A bank is a licensed financial institution that accepts money from people and businesses (as deposits) and then uses that money to give loans and other credit facilities.
➕ Added for exam
  • Legal definition – Banking Regulation Act, 1949, Section 5(b): “banking” means accepting, for the purpose of lending or investment, deposits of money from the public, repayable on demand or otherwise, and withdrawable by cheque, draft, order or otherwise.
  • Banks get their licence from RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai under Section 22 of the Banking Regulation Act.

Accounts a bank offers

  • Banks offer various accounts where people can park their money safely: savings accounts, current (business) accounts and fixed / term deposits.
  • In return the bank may pay interest on these deposits and issue a passbook, cheque book or ATM card.

3Types of Deposit

जमा के प्रकार – the two families of deposit and their four accounts.

The deposit family tree

  • Demand Deposit (माँग जमा) → Saving Account (बचत खाता) and Current Account (चालू खाता).
  • Term Deposit (मियादी जमा) → Recurring Deposit Account (आवर्ती जमा खाता) and Fixed Deposit Account (सावधि जमा खाता).

Demand Depositsमाँग जमा

  • These offer high liquidity for everyday use, with no fixed maturity.
  • Ideal for transactions but earn lower interest.
➕ Added for exam
  • A current account is for businesses with many transactions; banks normally pay no interest on it and give an overdraft facility.
  • Savings + current deposits together are called CASACASACurrent Account + Savings Account depositsLow-cost funds for a bankCASA ratio = CASA ÷ total deposits – cheap money for a bank, so a high CASA ratio is good for it.

Term Depositsसावधि / मियादी जमा

  • Term deposits are interest-bearing bank accounts where you deposit a lump sum (FD) or instalments (RD) for a fixed period (term) until maturity.
  • They earn higher interest than demand deposits.
➕ Added for exam
  • FD = one lump sum for a fixed term (minimum tenure is usually 7 days). RD = a fixed amount every month for a fixed term.
  • Deposits in a bank are insured by DICGCDICGCDeposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee CorporationWholly owned subsidiary of RBIInsures bank deposits up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank (principal + interest).

5Self-test

Answer out loud first, then click to check.

Who introduced no-frills accounts, and when?

The Reserve Bank of India, in 2005.

In which year were no-frills accounts renamed, and to what?

2012 – Basic Savings Bank Deposit Accounts (BSBDA).

What minimum balance does a BSBD account need?

None – zero minimum balance and no initial deposit.

Who can open a BSBD account?

Any individual, irrespective of age or income.

How many free withdrawals does a BSBD holder get per month?

Maximum four, including ATM withdrawals and RTGS/NEFT/clearing/internet debits, SIs, EMIs.

Is there a limit on the number of deposits in a BSBD account?

No.

A customer with a savings account opens a BSBDA in the same bank. What must they do?

Close the other savings account within 30 days of opening the BSBDA.

BSBD limits taught in class: total credits per year, maximum balance, debits per month?

₹1 lakh a year; ₹50,000 at any time; ₹10,000 a month.

Which documents does the BSBD ad say are enough to open the account?

Aadhaar Card and PAN Card, or Form No. 60.

What is “RBI Kehta Hai”?

A public awareness initiative of the RBI.

Define a bank.

A financial institution that accepts deposits and provides loans & advances and other financial services.

Name the two types of deposit and their examples.

Demand deposit (savings, current) and term deposit (fixed, recurring).

Which deposit offers higher liquidity, and which offers higher interest?

Demand deposits – higher liquidity; term deposits – higher interest.

What may happen if you withdraw a term deposit early?

You may pay a penalty.

Is a Recurring Deposit a demand or term deposit?

Term deposit.

What does a bank give a depositor besides interest?

A passbook, cheque book or ATM card.

⚡ Rapid Revision

  • No-frills account: RBI 2005, zero/low balance, for low-income groups → renamed BSBDA in 2012.
  • BSBDA: anyone, any age/income · no initial deposit · no minimum balance · free ATM-cum-debit card · unlimited deposits · 4 free withdrawals/month.
  • BSBDA holder: no other SB account in same bank; close existing one within 30 days.
  • Limits taught: credits ≤ ₹1 lakh/yr · balance ≤ ₹50,000 · debits ≤ ₹10,000/month.
  • RBI Kehta Hai = RBI’s public awareness initiative; BSBD ad: Aadhaar + PAN or Form 60.
  • Bank = accepts deposits + gives loans & advances (+ other services); licensed by RBI.
  • Demand deposit = Savings + Current: any-time withdrawal, high liquidity, low interest.
  • Term deposit = FD + RD: fixed term, higher interest, penalty on early withdrawal.
