1Recap: Payments Bank & Small Finance Bank

The opening slides redraw Class 3 as charts.

Payments Bank in three charts

  • Accepts deposits? → Demand deposits (current & savings accounts) yes; term deposits not permitted.
  • Deposit limit: ₹2 lakh per customer.
  • A payments bank does three things: facilitates deposits, gives payment services, and gives no loans.
  • Mind map: capital ₹100 crore · 25% branches in rural areas · debit card yes · loans no · FDI – 74%.
⚠️ Exam trapClass 3 said foreign shareholding is “as per FDI rules in private banks”. This slide gives the number: 74%, the FDI cap for private-sector banks.

Small Finance Bank Structure

  • Operational scope: offer both deposits and loans.
  • Capital requirement: a minimum of ₹300 crore to open a bank.
  • Foreign investment: allowed as per FDI rules.
  • Experience requirement: 10 years of banking experience.
  • Lending mandate: 60% of credit must go to the priority sector.

🎯 Likely QThe first banks

  • The Bank of Hindustan, established in 1770 in Calcutta by Alexander and Company, is considered the first bank in India.
  • Other early banks like the General Bank of India (1786) followed, but the Bank of Hindustan was the first to start operations.
  • The first bank purely managed by Indians was Punjab National Bank, established in Lahore in 1894.
  • The first Indian commercial bank wholly owned and managed by Indians was Central Bank of India, established in 1911 – known as the first truly Swadeshi bank.
  • Bank of India was the first Indian bank to open a branch outside India – in London, in 1946.
⚠️ Exam trapThree different “firsts”: PNB (1894) = first managed purely by Indians; Central Bank of India (1911) = first wholly owned and managed by Indians (Swadeshi); Bank of India (1946) = first branch abroad.

What were Presidency Banks?प्रेसिडेंसी बैंक

  • Presidency Banks were the earliest modern banking institutions set up in India during British colonial rule.
  • They were called Presidency banks because each was associated with one of the major British administrative regions – the Presidencies (Bengal, Bombay and Madras).

🏦 BankingImperial Bank of India → State Bank of India

  • In 1921, the Presidency Banks were merged to form the Imperial Bank of India. It was a private entity.
  • In 1955, the Imperial Bank of India was nationalised and renamed the State Bank of India, on the recommendation of the A.D. Gorwala Committee.
➕ Added for exam
  • SBI came into being on 1 July 1955 under the State Bank of India Act, 1955. The Gorwala Committee was the All India Rural Credit Survey Committee.
  • Until RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai was set up in 1935, the Imperial Bank also did some central-banking work for the Government.

Associate Banks of SBI (1959)

  • The State Bank of India (Subsidiary Banks) Act of 1959 enabled the formation of eight associate banks as subsidiaries of SBI.
  • This was done mainly by taking over banks of former princely states, to expand rural banking during India’s First Five-Year Plan.
  • 8 subsidiaries were attached with SBI in 1959 and named Associate Banks of SBI: State Bank of Bikaner, Jaipur, Saurashtra, Indore, Hyderabad, Patiala, Mysore and Travancore.

🎯 Likely QWhat happened to the associate banks

  • 1963 – State Bank of Bikaner and State Bank of Jaipur merged with each other to form State Bank of Bikaner and Jaipur (SBBJ).
  • 2008 – State Bank of Saurashtra merged into SBI.
  • 2010 – State Bank of Indore merged into SBI.
  • 1 April 2017 – the remaining 5 associate banks merged into SBI: State Bank of Bikaner and Jaipur, Hyderabad, Mysore, Patiala and Travancore.
  • Along with them, Bharatiya Mahila Bank was also merged into SBI.
⚠️ Exam trap8 associates in 1959 but only 5 merged in 2017: Bikaner + Jaipur had already become one bank (1963), and Saurashtra (2008) and Indore (2010) had gone earlier. 8 → 7 → 6 → 5.

4Merger of Banks

From 27 public sector banks in 2017 to 12 today.

Mergers before 2020

  • New Bank of India was merged into Punjab National Bank on 4 September 1993.
  • Vijaya Bank and Dena Bank merged with Bank of Baroda on 1 April 2019.
➕ Added for exam
  • New Bank of India (nationalised in 1980) is the only nationalised bank to have been merged into another before 2017.

🎯 Likely QThe mega-merger of 1 April 2020

  • Ten public sector banks were amalgamated into four banks from 1 April 2020.
  • Oriental Bank of Commerce (OBC) and United Bank of India → merged into Punjab National Bank.
  • Syndicate Bank → merged into Canara Bank.
  • Allahabad Bank → merged with Indian Bank.
  • Andhra Bank and Corporation Bank → merged with Union Bank of India.

🎯 Likely Q12 PSBs after the mergers

  • After the merger there are 12 PSBs – six merged banks and six independent public sector banks.
  • Six merged banks: SBI, Bank of Baroda, Punjab National Bank, Canara Bank, Union Bank of India, Indian Bank.
  • Six independent banks: Indian Overseas Bank, UCO Bank, Bank of Maharashtra, Punjab and Sind Bank, Bank of India, Central Bank of India.
