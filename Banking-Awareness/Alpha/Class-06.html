1History of the RBI

भारतीय रिज़र्व बैंक – from a Royal Commission to a nationalised central bank.

Timeline

  1. 1920sHilton-Young Commission (Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance) set up
  2. 1926The commission recommends a central bank for India
  3. 1934RBI Act, 1934 passed → RBI is a statutory body
  4. 1 Apr 1935RBI starts operations · paid-up capital ₹5 crore · central office in Calcutta · Governor Osborne Smith
  5. 1937Central office moves permanently to Mumbai
  6. 1943C. D. Deshmukh – first Indian Governor
  7. 1 Jan 1949RBI nationalised – privately owned until then

🎯 Likely QHow RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai came into being

  • The Hilton-Young Commission was the Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance, set up by the British Government of India in the 1920s.
  • In 1926, this commission recommended that the government create a central bank in the country.
  • RBI started its operations from 1 April 1935.
  • It was established via the RBI Act, 1934, so it is also known as a statutory body.
  • RBI was established in 1935 with an initial paid-up capital of ₹5 crore.
  • Initially the central office was in Calcutta, but it permanently moved to Mumbai in 1937.
  • RBI was nationalised on 1 January 1949. Initially it was privately owned, then it was owned by the Government of India.
➕ Added for exam
  • Nationalisation was done under the Reserve Bank of India (Transfer to Public Ownership) Act, 1948.
  • RBI’s emblem shows a tiger and a palm tree. Its financial year has run April–March since 2020-21 (it was July–June before).

RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai offices

  • RBI has 34 offices (regional offices and sub-offices) across India.

केंद्रीय बोर्ड और स्थानीय बोर्ड – who runs the RBI.

🎯 Likely QCentral Board of Directorsकेंद्रीय निदेशक बोर्ड

  • The Reserve Bank’s affairs are governed by a Central Board of Directors.
  • The board is appointed by the Government of India in keeping with the RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai Act.

  • The Central Board has 21 members:

  • Governor and Deputy Governors are full-time: term up to 5 years, eligible for reappointment.

Local Boardsस्थानीय बोर्ड

  • Constituted for the Western, Eastern, Northern and Southern Areas.
  • They consist of five members each.
  • Members are appointed by the Central Government and hold office for a term of four years.
  • 4 Local Boards: Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata.

5Note Printing Presses, Mints & Currency Issue

नोट मुद्रण प्रेस – who prints, who mints, who issues.

🎯 Likely QFour note printing pressesनोट मुद्रण प्रेस

  • Indian currency notes are printed at four secure presses managed by SPMCIL and BRBNMPL: Nashik (Maharashtra), Dewas (Madhya Pradesh), Mysore (Karnataka), Salboni (West Bengal).
  • Two presses are owned by the Government of India (SPMCIL) and two by the Reserve Bank through its wholly owned subsidiary BRBNMPL.
  • Government-owned: Nashik (Western India) and Dewas (Central India).
  • RBI-owned: Mysore (Southern India) and Salboni (Eastern India).

🎯 Likely QWho issues what

  • Government of India issues coins and the ₹1 note.
  • Reserve Bank of India issues ₹2 and above notes.
  • RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai can issue bank notes up to a maximum denomination of ₹10,000 as per the RBI Act, 1934.
  • As per the Coinage Act, 2011, coins can be of a maximum denomination of ₹1,000.

SPMCILभारत प्रतिभूति मुद्रण एवं निर्माण निगम

  • Security Printing & Minting Corporation of India Ltd is a Central Public Sector Enterprise (CPSE) under the Ministry of Finance.
  • Founded: 13 January 2006 · Headquarters: New Delhi.
  • It operates 9 production units: 4 mints, 2 currency note presses, 2 security printing presses and 1 security paper mill.
  • Coins are minted in four mints owned by the Government: Mumbai (Maharashtra), Kolkata (West Bengal), Hyderabad (Telangana), Noida (Uttar Pradesh).
  • India Security Press (ISP) – Nashik, Maharashtra · Security Printing Press (SPP) – Hyderabad, Telangana · Security Paper Mill – Narmadapuram, Madhya Pradesh.
➕ Added for exam
  • The two SPMCIL currency note presses are the Currency Note Press, Nashik and the Bank Note Press, Dewas. Narmadapuram was earlier called Hoshangabad.

6RBI’s Functions

आरबीआई के कार्य – seven roles.

🎯 Likely QSeven functions

  • Maker of Monetary Policy · Issuer of Currency · Banker to the Government · Banker to Banks · Foreign Exchange Management · Controller of Credit · Lender of Last Resort (LOLR).

Maker of Monetary Policy

  • Under the RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai Act, 1934 (as amended in 2016), RBI conducts monetary policy with the primary objective of price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth.
  • Fiscal policy is formulated by the Ministry of Finance, Government of India.
➕ Added for exam
  • The 2016 amendment brought in flexible inflation targeting (CPICPIConsumer Price IndexMeasures retail inflationReleased by NSO, MoSPIRBI target 4% (±2%) 4% ± 2%) and the Monetary Policy Committee – taught in the Monetary Policy classes.

Issuer of Currency

  • The Reserve Bank is the nation’s sole note issuing authority.
  • The Government of India is the issuing authority of coins and supplies coins to RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai on demand.
  • RBI puts the coins into circulation on behalf of the Central Government.

🎯 Likely QBanker to the Government

  • RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai is the banker of the Central Government:
  • — Section 20: RBI must accept and hold the Central Government’s money.
  • — Section 21: RBI can make payments and manage all banking work for the Government.
  • RBI is the banker of the State Governments / UTs:
  • — Section 21A: RBI acts as banker to State Governments by agreement.
  • — All States / UTs use RBI as banker except Sikkim.

Banker to Banks

  • The current accounts of banks are opened in e-Kuber (the CBS of RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai).
  • RBI gives short-term loans and advances to select banks, when necessary, to facilitate lending to specific sectors and purposes.

Foreign Exchange Management

  • RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai issues licences to banks and other institutions to act as Authorised Dealers in the foreign exchange market.
  • RBI is the custodian of the country’s foreign exchange reserves and manages their investment.
➕ Added for exam
  • RBI manages forex under FEMA, 1999 (which replaced FERA, 1973).

Controller of Credit

  • RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai uses monetary-policy tools to regulate money supply, control inflation and foster growth.
  • Quantitative methods: repo rate, CRR · Qualitative / selective measures: margin requirements, moral suasion.

Lender of Last Resort (LOLR)

  • RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai is called the lender of last resort for banks.
  • If a bank is healthy but faces a temporary cash shortage, RBI gives emergency funds.
  • This help is given when no other bank is willing to lend.
  • Purpose: to protect depositors’ money. It prevents the bank from failing and protects the banking system and financial stability.

8Minimum Reserve System

न्यूनतम भण्डारण प्रणाली – the backing for currency RBI issues.

🎯 Likely QMinimum Reserve System

  • Under this system, RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai should have a minimum reserve of ₹200 crore.
  • Of which gold ₹115 crore and ₹85 crore in foreign exchange.
  • RBI issues currency on the basis of the Minimum Reserve System.
➕ Added for exam
  • India adopted the Minimum Reserve System in 1957, replacing the Proportional Reserve System (1935–1956). The rule is in Section 33 of the RBI Act.
⚠️ Exam trap₹200 crore = ₹115 crore gold + ₹85 crore forex. Don’t mix with RBI’s ₹5 crore initial paid-up capital.
