🎯 Likely QScheduled Commercial Bank (SCB)अनुसूचित वाणिज्यिक बैंक

  • Scheduled commercial banks are banks included in the Second Schedule of the RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai Act, 1934 that carry out the normal business of banking – accepting deposits, giving loans and other banking services.
  • To be included in this Schedule, a bank must satisfy the conditions in Section 42(6)(a) of the RBI Act.
  • Every SCB gets two main facilities: eligibility to borrow from the RBI, and automatic membership of the clearing house, which allows cheque clearing.
  • SCBs include: Public Sector Banks, Private Sector Banks, Foreign Banks, Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), Scheduled Payments Banks, Scheduled Small Finance Banks and Scheduled Co-operative Banks.
➕ Added for exam
  • Section 42(6)(a) asks for paid-up capital and reserves of at least ₹5 lakh, and that the bank’s affairs are not run against depositors’ interests. A scheduled bank must keep its CRR with RBI (Section 42(1)).
  • A bank not in the Second Schedule is a non-scheduled bank (e.g. some Local Area Banks).

🏦 BankingPublic Sector Bankसार्वजनिक क्षेत्र का बैंक

  • Public Sector Banks are owned (majority stake of 51% or more) by the Government of India.
  • They are established under the State Bank of India Act, 1955 and the Banking Companies (Acquisition and Transfer of Undertakings) Acts of 1970 & 1980.

Private Sector Bankनिजी क्षेत्र का बैंक

  • Owned (majority stake of 51% or more) by private individuals or companies.
  • Licensed under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.

Foreign Bankविदेशी बैंक

  • Foreign banks are headquartered outside India but operate branches in India.
  • They must follow the rules of the Reserve Bank of India and the regulations of their parent bank abroad.

🏦 BankingRegional Rural Banks (RRBs)क्षेत्रीय ग्रामीण बैंक

  • Established under the Regional Rural Banks Act, 1976.
  • Meant to provide credit to farmers, rural workers and small entrepreneurs.

Small Finance Banks & Payments Banks

  • Both are licensed under the Banking Regulation Act, 1949.

🎯 Likely QLocal Area Banks (LABs)स्थानीय क्षेत्र बैंक

  • Local Area Banks were introduced in 1996 to operate in 2–3 contiguous districts, mobilise rural savings and improve local credit availability.
  • The Local Area Bank Scheme was introduced in August 1996. The Finance Minister referred to new private local banks with jurisdiction over two or three contiguous districts.
  • He observed this would let local institutions mobilise rural savings and make them available for investment in the local areas.
  • RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai announced guidelines for setting up LABs in the private sector on 24 August 1996.
  • Coastal Bank is the first Local Area Bank licensed by RBI, in 1999; it began operations on 27 December 1999. Address: Coastal Local Area Bank Ltd, Vijayawada (Andhra Pradesh).
  • KBS Bank (Krishna Bhima Samruddhi Local Area Bank) is one of the LABs operating now. Address: Mahabubnagar, Telangana.
  • RBI cancelled the licence of Maharashtra-based Subhadra Local Area Bank.
  • Capital Small Finance Bank started operations as India’s 1st Small Finance Bank on 24 April 2016, after conversion from Capital Local Area Bank.
  • Before conversion, Capital Local Area Bank had been India’s largest LAB since 14 January 2000.
➕ Added for exam
  • Minimum capital for a LAB was ₹5 crore. Finance Minister in 1996: P. Chidambaram (Budget speech, July 1996).
⚠️ Exam trapCoastal = first LAB (1999). Capital LAB = largest LAB, later the first SFB (2016). Subhadra = licence cancelled. KBS = still running.

🏦 BankingCo-operative Banksसहकारी बैंक

  • Co-operative banks are owned and managed by their members, set up mainly to provide rural and small-scale credit.
  • They are registered under the Co-operative Societies Act, 1912 (in India, under the State Co-operative Societies Acts).
  • They are regulated by the Reserve Bank of India and NABARDNABARDNational Bank for Agriculture and Rural DevelopmentEst. 12 July 1982HQ MumbaiMinistry of Finance under the relevant banking laws.
  • They come under RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai’s regulatory ambit under two laws: the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 and the Banking Laws (Co-operative Societies) Act.
  • The co-operative banking system came into being to promote saving and investment habits, especially in rural parts of the country.
  • Broadly, co-operative banks are divided into two categories – Urban and Rural.
  • Rural co-operatives are sub-divided into State Co-operative Banks, District Central Co-operative Banks and Primary Agricultural Credit Societies.
  • Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs) are of two kinds – Multi-State and those operating in a single state.
  • Three tiers named on the slide: Primary Credit Societies → Central Co-operative Bank → State Co-operative Bank.
⚠️ Exam trapThe slide writes “Banking Laws (Co-operative Societies) Act, 1955”. The real Act is the Banking Laws (Application to Co-operative Societies) Act, 1965. Pick 1965 if both appear.

Structure of Co-operative Credit Institutions (RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai chart)

  • Co-operative Credit Institutions → Urban Co-operative Banks and Rural Co-operative Credit Institutions.
  • Rural → Short-term structure: State Co-operative Banks → (District) Central Co-operative Banks → Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS).
  • Rural → Long-term structure: State Co-operative Agriculture and Rural Development Banks (SCARDBs) → Primary Co-operative Agriculture and Rural Development Banks (PCARDBs).

Land Development Bank (LDB)

  • A Land Development Bank (LDB) is a special kind of bank in India.
  • Its main objective is to promote the development of land, agriculture and increase agricultural production.
  • The first LDB was started at Jhang in Punjab in 1920.
➕ Added for exam
  • LDBs give long-term loans and are the old name of the long-term co-operative structure (today’s SCARDBs and PCARDBs). Jhang is now in Pakistan.

2All India Financial Institutions (AIFIs)

अखिल भारतीय वित्तीय संस्थान – the five RBI-regulated development finance institutions.

🎯 Likely QWhat AIFIs are and why they were created

  • AIFIs are specialised financial institutions set up by the Government of India to provide long-term finance to specific sectors of the economy.
  • They do not perform full commercial banking, like accepting regular deposits from the public.
  • Objectives: provide long-term credit · support priority and development sectors · promote economic and financial development · fill gaps not served by commercial banks.

3NABARD

National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development.

🎯 Likely QNABARDNABARDNational Bank for Agriculture and Rural DevelopmentEst. 12 July 1982HQ MumbaiMinistry of Finance – what it is

  • NABARD is an apex regulatory body for overall regulation of regional rural banks and apex co-operative banks in India.
  • It is under the jurisdiction of the Ministry of Finance, Government of India.
  • Established in 1982 under an Act of Parliament to promote sustainable and equitable agriculture and rural development.
  • It is a fully owned Government of India entity.
  • Formation: 12 July 1982 · Headquarters: Mumbai, Maharashtra · Chairman: Shaji K V.
➕ Added for exam
  • Set up under the NABARD Act, 1981, on the recommendation of the CRAFICARD (below). It took over the agricultural credit work of RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai and the Agricultural Refinance and Development Corporation.

CRAFICARD – the committee behind NABARDNABARDNational Bank for Agriculture and Rural DevelopmentEst. 12 July 1982HQ MumbaiMinistry of Finance

  • The Government formed a Committee to Review the Arrangements for Institutional Credit for Agriculture and Rural Development (CRAFICARD).
  • It was set up on 30 March 1979 under the chairmanship of B. Sivaraman, former Member of the Planning Commission.

🎯 Likely QNABARDNABARDNational Bank for Agriculture and Rural DevelopmentEst. 12 July 1982HQ MumbaiMinistry of Finance’s subsidiaries

➕ Added for exam

4National Housing Bank (NHB)

राष्ट्रीय आवास बैंक

🎯 Likely QNHBNHBNational Housing BankEst. 1988HQ New DelhiRegulates and refinances housing finance companies

  • NHB is the apex regulatory body for overall regulation and licensing of housing finance companies in India.
  • It is under the jurisdiction of the Ministry of Finance, Government of India.
  • It was set up on 9 July 1988 under the National Housing Bank Act, 1987.
  • It was set up on the recommendation of the C. Rangarajan Committee.
  • RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai had 100% shareholding in NHB, which was divested for ₹1,450 crore.

  • Founded: 9 July 1988 · Headquarters: New Delhi · Managing Director: Sanjay Shukla.

5SIDBI

Small Industries Development Bank of India.

🎯 Likely QSIDBI

  • SIDBI is the apex regulatory body for overall licensing and regulation of micro, small and medium enterprise finance companies in India.
  • It was set up on 2 April 1990 under an Act of Indian Parliament.
  • It is under the jurisdiction of the Ministry of Finance, Government of India.
  • SIDBI was established by the Government of India as a wholly owned subsidiary of IDBI Bank.
  • It was delinked from IDBI with effect from 27 March 2000.
  • Formed: 2 April 1990 · Headquarters: Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh · Chairman and Managing Director: Manoj Mittal.
  • Shareholding: Government of India 20.85% · State Bank of India 15.65% · LIC 13.33% · NABARDNABARDNational Bank for Agriculture and Rural DevelopmentEst. 12 July 1982HQ MumbaiMinistry of Finance 9.36% · others 40.81%.
⚠️ Exam trapThe English slide wrongly prints “Headquarters: Manoj Mittal”. The Hindi slide has it right: HQ is Lucknow. Manoj Mittal is the CMD.

7NaBFID

National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development – the newest AIFI.

🎯 Likely QNaBFID – basics

  • NaBFID is a Development Finance Institution (DFI) in India, founded on 19 April 2021.
  • It was set up to support the development of long-term infrastructure financing in India.
  • It is established under the National Bank for Financing Infrastructure and Development Act, 2021. Established: 2021 · Headquarters: Mumbai.
  • NaBFID is regulated and supervised by RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai as an All India Financial Institution (AIFI) under Sections 45L and 45N of the RBI Act, 1934.
  • It is the fifth AIFI, after EXIM BankEXIM BankExport-Import Bank of IndiaEst. 1982HQ MumbaiExtends Lines of Credit to other countries, NABARDNABARDNational Bank for Agriculture and Rural DevelopmentEst. 12 July 1982HQ MumbaiMinistry of Finance, NHBNHBNational Housing BankEst. 1988HQ New DelhiRegulates and refinances housing finance companies and SIDBI.

NaBFID – leadership

  • Chairman: K. V. Kamath · Managing Director: Rajkiran Rai G.
  • The Chairperson, MD and Deputy MDs hold office for not more than five years, and can be re-appointed subject to an overall term not exceeding ten years.

🎯 Likely QNaBFID – capital structure

  • Initially, the Government of India infused capital of ₹20,000 crore.
  • The entire shareholding is currently held by the Government of India.
  • The authorised share capital is ₹1 lakh crore, divided into ten thousand crore fully paid-up shares of ten rupees each.
  • The Central Government granted (contributed) ₹5,000 crore to the institution.
  • Shares may be held by the Central Government, multilateral institutions, sovereign wealth funds, pension funds, insurers, financial institutions, banks, and others as prescribed.
  • The Central Government shall hold at least 26% of the shares at all times.
⚠️ Exam trapThree NaBFID money figures: ₹20,000 crore initial capital · ₹5,000 crore grant · ₹1 lakh crore authorised capital. Government floor: 26%.

8IFCI

Industrial Finance Corporation of India – India’s first DFI, not an AIFI today.

IFCI Ltd

  • IFCI Ltd was established in 1948 as a statutory corporation (then called Industrial Finance Corporation of India) to provide medium- and long-term finance to industries.
  • In 1993, after the Industrial Finance Corporation Act was repealed, IFCI became a Public Limited Company registered under the Companies Act, 1956.
  • Today IFCI is registered with RBIRBIReserve Bank of IndiaEst. 1 April 1935 (RBI Act 1934)Nationalised 1949HQ Mumbai as a Systemically Important Non-Deposit Taking NBFC (NBFC-ND-SI), and is a Public Financial Institution (PFI) under Section 2(72) of the Companies Act, 2013.
  • Headquarters: New Delhi.
⚠️ Exam trapIFCI (1948) is the first development finance institution of India, but it is now an NBFC, not one of the five RBI-regulated AIFIs.

⚡ Rapid Revision

  • Types: Central (RBI) · Commercial · Specialised · Co-operative.
  • Scheduled bank = RBI Act 1934 Second Schedule, Sec 42(6)(a); gets RBI borrowing + clearing-house membership.
  • PSB: govt ≥ 51% (SBI Act 1955; Acquisition Acts 1970 & 1980) · Private: ≥ 51% private, BR Act 1949 · Foreign: HQ abroad, follows RBI + parent.
  • RRB Act 1976 · SFB & PB: BR Act 1949, financial inclusion · LAB: Aug 1996, 2–3 districts; Coastal first (27 Dec 1999); Capital LAB → first SFB (24 Apr 2016).
  • Co-op: members own; Co-operative Societies Act 1912; RBI + NABARD; urban (multi-state / single-state) vs rural (StCB → DCCB → PACS; long-term SCARDB → PCARDB). LDB: Jhang 1920.
  • AIFIs (5): EXIM 1982 Mumbai · NABARD 12 Jul 1982 Mumbai · NHB 9 Jul 1988 New Delhi · SIDBI 2 Apr 1990 Lucknow · NaBFID 19 Apr 2021 Mumbai.
  • NABARD: CRAFICARD (B. Sivaraman, 30 Mar 1979); ₹100 cr → ₹30,000 cr; RBI exit Oct 2010 + 26 Feb 2019; 7 subsidiaries.
  • NHB: Rangarajan Committee; RBI 100% sold for ₹1,450 cr; HFC regulation → RBI (Finance Act 2019).
  • NaBFID: Sec 45L & 45N; ₹20,000 cr + ₹5,000 cr grant; authorised ₹1 lakh cr; govt ≥ 26%; Kamath / Rajkiran Rai G; term ≤ 5 yrs (max 10).
  • IFCI 1948 → company 1993 → NBFC-ND-SI + PFI (Sec 2(72), Companies Act 2013), HQ New Delhi.
