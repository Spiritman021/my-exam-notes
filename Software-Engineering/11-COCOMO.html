Chapter 11

COCOMO Model

COCOMO, the Constructive Cost Model, is the heuristic estimation technique from chapter 10 turned into equations. You supply the estimated size in KLOC and the type of project, and it returns the effort in person-months and the development time in months. It was published by Barry Boehm in 1981 (Software Engineering Economics) and comes in three levels of detail: Basic, Intermediate and Complete (Detailed). COCOMO numericals are among the most predictable marks in the exam.

11.2Basic COCOMO: Effort, Development Time, Person-Month and Productivity

🎓 From the lecture
Effort E = a × (KLOC)^b person-months (PM) Dev. time Tdev = c × (E)^d months Productivity P = KLOC / E (or LOC / E) Avg. staff SS = E / Tdev persons
The constants depend on the category. They are usually given in the exam, but memorise them anyway:
Modeabcd
Organic2.41.052.50.38
Semi-detached3.01.122.50.35
Embedded3.61.202.50.32
Person-month (PM) is the area under the curve of number of people plotted against time. 10 PM can mean 10 people for 1 month, 5 people for 2 months, and so on.
⚠️ Exam trap
  • The transcript's speech-to-text reads the d constants as "38, 35, 32". They are 0.38, 0.35, 0.32.
  • c = 2.5 for all three modes.
  • Because b > 1, effort grows more than linearly with size (diseconomy of scale).
  • Development time is almost the same for all three modes at a given size, because a larger effort is spread over a larger team.
  • Always convert LOC to KLOC before applying the formula: 50,000 LOC = 50 KLOC.

11.3Basic COCOMO Worked Example (50 KLOC Semi-Detached)

🎓 From the lecture A semi-detached project of 50,000 LOC: find effort, time and productivity. The lecture arrives at about 17 months. It says the effort figure two ways ("329", then "239") and quotes productivity as "209". The computation below settles it: effort ≈ 240 PM, and "209" is productivity in LOC per person-month.
Worked example · Basic COCOMO, semi-detached, 50 KLOC
E = 3.0 × (50)^1.12 = 3.0 × 79.96 ≈ 239.9 PM Tdev = 2.5 × (239.9)^0.35 = 2.5 × 6.81 ≈ 17.0 months SS = 239.9 / 17.0 ≈ 14 persons P = 50,000 / 239.9 ≈ 208 LOC/PM (= 0.208 KLOC/PM)
Same size, all three modes (50 KLOC)

Effort rises steeply from organic to embedded while time barely moves. Harder projects need more people, not more months.

➕ Added for exam Cost = effort × monthly cost per person. For the 50 KLOC semi-detached project at ₹1,00,000 per person-month: 239.9 × 1,00,000 ≈ ₹2.4 crore. Exams also ask the reverse: "If the project must finish in 10 months, how many people?" Answer: E / T. Remember, though, that COCOMO's nominal time is the optimal schedule. Compressing it raises effort (the SCED cost driver in Intermediate COCOMO).

11.4Intermediate COCOMO: Cost Drivers and EAF

🎓 From the lecture Basic COCOMO wrongly assumes that effort and time depend on size alone. In reality, required reliability, team skill, tools and hardware limits matter too. Intermediate COCOMO adds cost-driver attributes in four groups (product, personnel, project, computer). Each is rated from very low to very high using a table, and the ratings are multiplied into an Effort Adjustment Factor (EAF):
E = a × (KLOC)^b × EAF
Lecture example: an embedded project of 30 KLOC with high reliability required (RELY = high, multiplier 1.15) gives effort ≈ 191 PM and time ≈ 13 months.
Worked example · Intermediate COCOMO, embedded, 30 KLOC, RELY high Intermediate COCOMO uses its own a constants: organic 3.2, semi-detached 3.0, embedded 2.8. The exponents are unchanged (1.05, 1.12, 1.20).
Nominal E = 2.8 × (30)^1.20 = 2.8 × 59.23 ≈ 165.9 PM EAF = 1.15 (only RELY differs from nominal 1.00) E = 165.9 × 1.15 ≈ 190.7 PM Tdev = 2.5 × (190.7)^0.32 ≈ 13.4 months
This matches the lecture's ≈191 PM and ≈13 months.
➕ Added for exam There are 15 cost drivers in four groups:
GroupCost drivers
ProductRELY (required reliability), DATA (database size), CPLX (product complexity)
Computer (hardware)TIME (execution-time constraint), STOR (main-storage constraint), VIRT (virtual machine volatility), TURN (turnaround time)
PersonnelACAP (analyst capability), AEXP (application experience), PCAP (programmer capability), VEXP (virtual machine experience), LEXP (language experience)
ProjectMODP (modern programming practices), TOOL (use of software tools), SCED (required schedule)
EAF = the product of all 15 multipliers; nominal = 1.00. Drivers that make the job harder (high RELY, high CPLX) have multipliers above 1. Drivers that help (high ACAP, high PCAP, good tools) have multipliers below 1. Typical EAF range: about 0.9 to 1.4.
⚠️ Exam trap Capability drivers work the opposite way to product drivers. High analyst capability lowers effort (multiplier < 1), while high required reliability raises it (multiplier > 1). The EAF is a product, not a sum.

11.5Complete (Detailed) COCOMO: Subsystem-wise Estimation

🎓 From the lecture Intermediate COCOMO still treats the whole project as one homogeneous category. Real large systems are heterogeneous, made of subsystems of different kinds. In a bank system, for example, the report module may be organic, the database layer semi-detached and the real-time transaction engine embedded. Complete COCOMO estimates each subsystem separately with its own mode and cost drivers, then adds them up to get the total effort and time.
➕ Added for exam Detailed COCOMO also uses phase-sensitive effort multipliers: each cost driver can have a different effect in each phase (requirements and design, detailed design, code and unit test, integration and test). It applies them over a three-level hierarchy: module, subsystem, system. It distributes effort and time across the phases rather than giving one total.

11.6COCOMO IIADDED

➕ Added for exam COCOMO II (2000) updated the model for modern development (reuse, object-oriented code, spiral and iterative processes). It has three sub-models used at different stages:
Sub-modelWhenSize measure
Application compositionPrototyping, GUI buildersObject points (screens, reports, 3GL components)
Early designRequirements stable, architecture being exploredFunction points
Post-architectureArchitecture fixed, during developmentKLOC or FP, with 17 cost drivers and 5 scale factors
In COCOMO II the exponent is not fixed per mode. It is computed from five scale factors: precedentedness, development flexibility, architecture/risk resolution, team cohesion and process maturity.

11.7Solved Exam QuestionsADDED

Q1COCOMO was proposed by: (a) Albrecht (b) Halstead (c) Boehm (d) Putnam
Answer: (c) Barry Boehm, 1981.
Q2An organic project is estimated at 20 KLOC. Find the effort and time using Basic COCOMO (a = 2.4, b = 1.05, c = 2.5, d = 0.38).
Answer: E = 2.4 × 20^1.05 ≈ 2.4 × 23.24 ≈ 55.8 PM; T = 2.5 × 55.8^0.38 ≈ 2.5 × 4.61 ≈ 11.5 months; staff ≈ 55.8 / 11.5 ≈ 5 persons.
Q3A semi-detached project is 20 KLOC. Find its effort.
Answer: E = 3.0 × 20^1.12 ≈ 3.0 × 28.65 ≈ 86 PM; T = 2.5 × 86^0.35 ≈ 11.9 months.
Q4An air-traffic control system belongs to which COCOMO mode?
Answer: Embedded (tight real-time and hardware constraints).
Q5In Intermediate COCOMO, the Effort Adjustment Factor is obtained by:
Answer: multiplying the ratings of the 15 cost drivers.
Q6Which COCOMO level estimates each subsystem separately and sums them?
Answer: Complete (Detailed) COCOMO.

⚡ Rapid Revision

  • COCOMO = Constructive Cost Model, Boehm 1981, heuristic, size in KLOC.
  • Modes: organic (small, simple, 2–50 KLOC) · semi-detached (medium, 50–300) · embedded (complex, tight constraints, >300).
  • E = a·KLOC^b (PM) · T = 2.5·E^d (months) · staff = E / T · productivity = KLOC / E.
  • Constants a/b/d: 2.4/1.05/0.38 · 3.0/1.12/0.35 · 3.6/1.20/0.32.
  • 50 KLOC semi-detached → E ≈ 240 PM, T ≈ 17 months, ≈ 14 people, ≈ 208 LOC/PM.
  • Intermediate: E = a·KLOC^b·EAF. 15 cost drivers in 4 groups (product, computer, personnel, project). a = 3.2 / 3.0 / 2.8. Example: 30 KLOC embedded, RELY 1.15 → ≈191 PM, ≈13 months.
  • Complete: per subsystem with its own mode, then sum; phase-sensitive multipliers.
  • COCOMO II: application composition (object points) · early design · post-architecture; scale factors set the exponent.
