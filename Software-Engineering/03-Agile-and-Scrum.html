Chapter 3

Agile Development and Scrum

The models in chapter 2 assume you can plan most of the project up front. Real clients change their minds, and markets move faster than a year-long plan. Agile answers this by building the product in short iterations, talking to the client constantly and treating change as normal. Scrum is the most widely used way of running an Agile team, and most IT companies today work in sprints.

3.1Agile Characteristics and Principles

🎓 From the lecture Characteristics: quick and accurate work, agility (speed of response), flexibility, collaboration and teamwork.

Principles

  • Frequent communication and collaboration are valued over documentation, formality and procedure: action over formality.
  • Focus on incremental product solutions: deliver working pieces early.
  • Value the client's ideas and requirements and act on them immediately.
  • Accept changes and stay flexible.
Iteration by iteration, the increments approach the final product.
➕ Added for exam The Agile Manifesto was written in 2001 by 17 developers at Snowbird, Utah. Its four values (the item on the left is valued more):
Valued moreover
Individuals and interactionsprocesses and tools
Working softwarecomprehensive documentation
Customer collaborationcontract negotiation
Responding to changefollowing a plan
The manifesto has 12 principles. The most-quoted: satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery; welcome changing requirements, even late; deliver working software frequently (weeks rather than months); business people and developers work together daily; face-to-face conversation is the most effective communication; working software is the primary measure of progress; sustainable pace; simplicity ("maximising the work not done"); self-organising teams; the team regularly reflects and adjusts.
⚠️ Exam trap Agile does not say "no documentation" or "no planning". It values the left-hand items more, while still seeing value in the right-hand items. The primary measure of progress is working software, not documents or hours spent.

3.2Team X (Waterfall) vs Team Y (Agile) Scenario

🎓 From the lecture Two teams receive the same project.
  • Team X follows the traditional waterfall: communication → planning → modeling → construction → deployment.
  • Team Y follows Agile, in short iterations.
Mid-project the client changes a requirement. Team X has already planned, designed and partly built around the old requirement, so it must go back to the start, wasting months and money. Team Y simply puts the change into the next iteration and carries on.

3.3Agile vs Evolutionary Model

🎓 From the lecture Both are incremental and iterative, so students often confuse them. The difference is attitude:
AgileEvolutionary
Values quickness and action over formalityFollows defined procedures, rules and steps
Minimal documentation, constant communicationFormal phases and documentation within each build
Best when deadlines are tight and change is frequentBest for large, complex projects where risk analysis matters

3.4Scrum Framework: Roles, Product Backlog, Sprint Planning and Sprint Backlog

🎓 From the lecture Scrum is a framework, not a model. It is Agile-based: teamwork, frequent communication, fast delivery, efficient in cost and time. It is widely used in companies.
  • Sprint: a time box of one month or less, typically 1, 2 or 4 weeks.
  • Product Owner: represents the client and owns the list of what to build.
  • Product Backlog: the prioritised list of all features the product needs, marked high, medium or low priority.
  • Sprint Planning: the team picks the high-priority items it can finish this sprint and estimates the time, resources, effort and cost.
  • Sprint Backlog: the subset of the product backlog chosen for the current sprint.

Backlog items are usually written as user stories ("As a <user>, I want <goal> so that <benefit>") and sized in story points. The story points completed per sprint give the team's velocity.

3.5Sprint, Daily Scrum, PSPI, Sprint Review and Retrospective

🎓 From the lecture The full cycle:
  1. Product Owner → Product Backlog (all features, prioritised).
  2. Sprint Planning → Sprint Backlog (the high-priority subset).
  3. The sprint runs for 1–4 weeks. Every day there is a Daily Scrum, a short 15–20 minute meeting led by the Scrum Master, about progress, obstacles and challenges.
  4. The sprint produces a PSPI (Potentially Shippable Product Increment): a working piece of the product, though not necessarily deployed yet.
  5. Sprint Review: the increment is shown to the stakeholders for feedback.
  6. Sprint Retrospective: the team looks back at what went well and what went badly, and takes the lessons into the next sprint.
  7. Back to Sprint Planning for the next sprint.
⚠️ Exam trap
  • The Scrum Guide time-boxes the Daily Scrum to 15 minutes; the lecture's "15–20 minutes" is loose. It is an event for the Developers. The Scrum Master makes sure it happens and stays within 15 minutes but does not have to run it.
  • Sprint Review inspects the product (what was built). Sprint Retrospective inspects the process (how the team worked). The retrospective comes after the review and is the last event of the sprint.
  • The Product Owner, not the Scrum Master, orders the Product Backlog.
  • Sprint length stays fixed. A sprint can be cancelled only by the Product Owner.
➕ Added for exam A burndown chart plots the work remaining (y-axis) against time (x-axis) during a sprint; the line should slope down to zero by sprint end. A burnup chart plots work completed rising toward the total scope.

3.6Other Agile Methods: XP, Kanban and MoreADDED

➕ Added for exam
MethodKey ideas
Extreme Programming (XP) (Kent Beck)Pair programming, test-driven development (TDD): write the test first, continuous integration, refactoring, small frequent releases, collective code ownership, simple design, an on-site customer, 40-hour week. Values: communication, simplicity, feedback, courage, respect.
KanbanA visual board (To Do → In Progress → Done) with WIP (work-in-progress) limits; continuous flow with no fixed sprints. It originated in Toyota's production system.
LeanEliminate waste, deliver fast, defer decisions, build quality in.
FDD (Feature-Driven Development)Builds by feature, from an overall model and a feature list.
DSDMFixed time and cost with flexible features; uses MoSCoW prioritisation (Must, Should, Could, Won't).
Crystal (Alistair Cockburn)A family of methods (Clear, Yellow, Orange…) chosen by team size and criticality.
ASD (Adaptive Software Development)Speculate → Collaborate → Learn cycle.
🎯 IBPS exam alert "Pair programming" and "test-first development" → XP. "WIP limits" → Kanban. "MoSCoW" → DSDM. "Sprint", "Scrum Master" → Scrum.

3.7Solved Exam QuestionsADDED

Q1According to the Agile Manifesto, which is valued more? (a) Comprehensive documentation (b) Following a plan (c) Customer collaboration (d) Processes and tools
Answer: (c) Customer collaboration (over contract negotiation).
Q2The maximum length of a Scrum sprint is:
Answer: one month.
Q3Who is responsible for ordering (prioritising) the Product Backlog?
Answer: the Product Owner.
Q4Which Scrum event inspects how the last sprint went with regard to people, processes and tools?
Answer: the Sprint Retrospective.
Q5Pair programming is a practice of: (a) Scrum (b) Kanban (c) Extreme Programming (d) Spiral
Answer: (c) XP.
Q6The primary measure of progress in Agile is:
Answer: working software.

⚡ Rapid Revision

  • Agile: quick, flexible, collaborative, incremental. Action over formality. Accept change.
  • Manifesto (2001): individuals & interactions, working software, customer collaboration, responding to change, each valued over processes & tools, documentation, contract negotiation, following a plan. 12 principles.
  • Team X (waterfall) restarts on a change; Team Y (Agile) puts it into the next iteration.
  • Agile vs Evolutionary: both incremental and iterative; Agile is informal and fast, Evolutionary is procedural.
  • Scrum is a framework. 3 roles: PO, SM, Developers · 5 events: Sprint, Planning, Daily (15 min), Review, Retro · 3 artifacts: Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment.
  • Sprint ≤ 1 month (1–4 weeks). PSPI = potentially shippable product increment.
  • Review = product; Retrospective = process (last event).
  • XP = pair programming + TDD + CI · Kanban = WIP limits · DSDM = MoSCoW.
