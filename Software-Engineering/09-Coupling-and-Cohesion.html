Chapter 9

Modularity, Coupling and Cohesion

Chapter 8 said a good design has "high cohesion and low coupling". This chapter explains what that means. Splitting software into modules is easy; splitting it well is the hard part. Two measures decide it. Coupling measures how much modules depend on each other, and should be low. Cohesion measures how strongly the parts inside one module belong together, and should be high. Exams love the ranked lists of their types.

9.1Modularity, Fan-In and Fan-Out

🎓 From the lecture Modularity (first met among the quality attributes in chapter 1) means splitting features, functions and data into separate modules.

Two simple counts describe a module's connections, using modules M1–M4: M1 calls M2 and M3, and both of them call M4.

  • Fan-out = the number of modules a module calls. M1 has fan-out 2 because it calls M2 and M3.
  • Fan-in = the number of modules that call it. M4 has fan-in 2 because M2 and M3 call it, and fan-out 0.
Fully independent modules, with no calls at all, would be ideal but are unrealistic: modules must cooperate.
➕ Added for exam High fan-in is good: a module called by many others is being reused, typically a utility such as "validate date". Very high fan-out is bad: a module calling too many others is doing too much coordinating and is hard to understand. A common guideline keeps fan-out at about 7 or fewer. A good structure is "fan-out high at the top, fan-in high at the bottom", shaped like an oval.

9.2Degree of Modularity

🎓 From the lecture How many modules should there be? Somewhere between 1 and n, so take the middle path. Too few modules means each one is huge and does everything. Too many means the interactions and management between them become tedious.

9.3Coupling vs Cohesion (Two Neighbouring Houses)

🎓 From the lecture Think of two neighbouring houses as two modules.
  • Coupling is between modules: how much the neighbours depend on each other, such as borrowing sugar, milk or salt. It should be low.
  • Cohesion is within a module: the bond between the family members inside one house. It should be high.
Coupling has three broad levels: uncoupled (no arrows between modules), loosely coupled (a few arrows) and highly coupled (neighbours in and out of each other's homes every day).
CouplingCohesion
MeasuresInterdependence between modules (inter-module)Relatedness of elements within a module (intra-module)
GoalLow (loose)High (strong)
Best typeData couplingFunctional cohesion
Worst typeContent couplingCoincidental cohesion
Why it mattersLow coupling → a change in one module doesn't ripple into othersHigh cohesion → each module has one clear job, easy to understand and reuse
➕ Added for exam Together, high cohesion and low coupling give functional independence (chapter 8). The two concepts were introduced by Larry Constantine (with Yourdon and Myers) in structured design in the 1970s. In general, as cohesion rises, coupling tends to fall.

9.4Types of Coupling: Data, Stamp, Control, External, Common, Content (Exam Hall)

🎓 From the lecture Six types, from best (lowest coupling) to worst (highest), each illustrated in an exam hall:
#TypeWhat is sharedExam-hall example
1Data (best)Only elementary data values are passed, like call by valueWhispering just the answer ("option B") to a friend
2StampA whole data structure (or its address) is passedHanding over your whole answer sheet when the friend needed one answer
3ControlOne module passes control information (a flag) that decides the other's logicEmotionally pressuring a friend for answers; Munna Bhai MBBS forcing the doctor to write his exam
4ExternalModules depend on something external: hardware, a device, a protocolDepending on the bus or cab to reach the exam hall
5CommonModules share global dataTwo friends sharing one scale, pencil and eraser, so one waits while the other uses it. It is like the critical section and race conditions in an OS.
6Content (worst)One module directly uses or modifies another's internal data or codeOne module is effectively part of another and changes its data directly
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Stamp coupling is defined as passing a composite data structure (a record or struct) when the receiver uses only part of it. The lecture links it to call by reference, but the key idea is "more data than needed". Passing only the fields required turns it into data coupling.
  • Control coupling involves a flag that tells the other module what to do (for example print(report, mode=2), which switches its logic).
  • Common = global variables. Content = jumping into, or modifying, another module's internals, such as a GOTO into the middle of another module.
  • Some books list no coupling (completely independent modules) before data coupling as the theoretical ideal.

9.5Types of Cohesion: Functional to Coincidental

🎓 From the lecture Cohesion measures the strength of the relationship between the functions inside one module, like the bond within a family. High is desired. Seven types, from best to worst:
#TypeElements are grouped because…Example module
1Functional (best)All of them work together on one single taskcalculate_salary()
2SequentialThe output of one is the input of the nextread data → validate → format
3CommunicationalThey operate on the same dataFrom one customer record: print the address and compute the balance
4ProceduralThey follow the steps of one procedure (order of execution), even if the data differscheck permission → open file → log the attempt
5TemporalThey run at the same time or in the same time spanSystem start-up: initialise variables, open files, clear the screen
6LogicalThey are logically similar (same category) but functionally different; one is picked by a flagAll input routines: read from keyboard, file or network
7Coincidental (worst)No relation, grouped at random, like strangers living in one houseutils.c holding print_date, sort_array and send_email
⚠️ Exam trap
  • Sequential vs communicational: sequential = output → input chain; communicational = same input data, no chain.
  • Procedural vs temporal: procedural = steps in a required order for one procedure; temporal = merely happen at the same time (initialisation, shutdown).
  • Logical vs coincidental: logical = same category ("all print functions"); coincidental = no relationship at all.
  • Do not mix up the ends of the two scales. The best coupling is data; the best cohesion is functional. The worst coupling is content; the worst cohesion is coincidental.
🎯 IBPS exam alert Typical questions:
  • "Which is the most desirable coupling?" → Data.
  • "Modules sharing global variables exhibit?" → Common coupling.
  • "A module that initialises all variables at system start-up has?" → Temporal cohesion.
  • "Arrange the cohesion types in increasing order of strength" → coincidental < logical < temporal < procedural < communicational < sequential < functional.

9.6Solved Exam QuestionsADDED

Q1Which is the worst (most undesirable) type of coupling? (a) Data (b) Control (c) Common (d) Content
Answer: (d) Content.
Q2Module A passes a flag to module B telling it whether to print or display. This is:
Answer: Control coupling.
Q3A module whose elements all contribute to computing a single result shows:
Answer: Functional cohesion (the best kind).
Q4A module calls 5 other modules and is called by 3. What are its fan-in and fan-out?
Answer: Fan-in = 3, fan-out = 5.
Q5In a module, the output of one element is the input to the next. The cohesion is:
Answer: Sequential.
Q6A good software design should have: (a) high coupling, high cohesion (b) low coupling, low cohesion (c) low coupling, high cohesion (d) high coupling, low cohesion
Answer: (c).

⚡ Rapid Revision

  • Fan-out = modules I call; fan-in = modules that call me. High fan-in = reuse (good); very high fan-out = bad.
  • Degree of modularity: choose the middle path, the minimum-cost region between too few and too many modules.
  • Coupling = between modules → low. Cohesion = within a module → high. (Neighbours borrowing sugar vs a family bond.)
  • Coupling, best → worst: Data → Stamp → Control → External → Common → Content.
  • Exam-hall examples: whisper the answer (data) · hand over the whole sheet (stamp) · emotional pressure, Munna Bhai (control) · bus or cab (external) · shared pencil and eraser (common) · one module inside another (content).
  • Cohesion, best → worst: Functional → Sequential → Communicational → Procedural → Temporal → Logical → Coincidental.
  • Global variables = common coupling · start-up initialisation = temporal cohesion · random utilities = coincidental cohesion.
