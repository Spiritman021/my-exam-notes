Capability Maturity Model (CMM)

Two companies can have equally talented programmers and still deliver very differently. One hits every deadline; the other lurches from crisis to crisis. The difference is usually the process: how predictably, measurably and repeatably the organisation builds software. The Capability Maturity Model rates that process on a five-step ladder and tells an organisation what to fix to climb to the next step.

7.1CMM as a Process-Improvement Framework (Dhoni's Process Mindset)

🎓 From the lecture CMM is a framework, not an SDLC model. It does not tell you the phases of a project; it focuses on improving and enhancing the software process of an organisation.

The lecture borrows M.S. Dhoni's mindset: "Focus on the process, and the results will follow." Good results come from a good process, so keep improving the process. CMM measures how mature that process is on five levels:

Initial → Repeatable → Defined → Managed → Optimized

7.2Level 1 Initial (Opening a New Shop)

🎓 From the lecture Like someone who has just opened a new shop or business: everything is new and nothing is settled.
  • Disorganised, inconsistent, unpredictable, chaotic, with little control.
  • Expectations are unrealistic.
  • No documentation.
➕ Added for exam At Level 1 the process is ad hoc; success depends on individual heroics, not on the organisation. The same team may succeed on one project and fail on the next. Schedules and budgets are usually overrun. Level 1 has no key process areas: it is the starting point every organisation has by default.

7.3Level 2 Repeatable (Gym Routine)

🎓 From the lecture Joining a gym: for the first 10 days you are irregular, then you settle into a fixed weekly routine (chest on Monday, back on Tuesday…).
  • Consistency and repeatable schedules appear: the same tasks are done the same way again and again.
  • Experience grows, so expectations become realistic.
  • Small incremental changes are made.
➕ Added for exam Level 2 introduces basic project management: tracking cost, schedule and functionality. Successes on similar past projects can be repeated. The discipline lives at the project level; the organisation does not yet have one standard process.

7.4Level 3 Defined

🎓 From the lecture A well-defined business:
  • Documentation for every task.
  • Standardisation and integration of processes.
  • Strict principles, and trust is maintained.
  • Risk management (identifying risks) begins.
➕ Added for exam At Level 3 there is one organisation-wide standard process, and each project tailors it. Both management and engineering activities are documented and integrated. Training programmes, peer reviews and an organisational process group are in place.

7.5Level 4 Managed

🎓 From the lecture
  • Both quantity and quality are managed.
  • From experience, budget and time are predictable, so deadlines and budgets are no longer overrun.
➕ Added for exam The key word for Level 4 is quantitative. Detailed measurements (metrics) of the process and the product are collected, and both are controlled using statistical data. Quality goals are set as numbers ("defect density below 0.5 per KLOC") and tracked.

7.6Level 5 Optimized (Continuous Improvement)

🎓 From the lecture The highest level, where the question is always "what next?"
  • Continuous Improvement Process (CIP).
  • Innovation, creativity and incremental updates.
➕ Added for exam At Level 5 the organisation uses the quantitative feedback from Level 4 to improve the process continuously, pilot new technologies, and prevent defects rather than just fix them. Many large Indian IT companies (TCS, Infosys, Wipro) have been appraised at Level 5.
⚠️ Exam trap The official CMM name for Level 5 is "Optimizing"; "Optimized", as in the lecture, also appears in exams. Each level is built on the previous one, and levels cannot be skipped. Risk management appears at Level 3 (Defined), not at Level 2.

7.7Key Process Areas, CMMI and ISO 9001ADDED

➕ Added for exam CMM was developed by the Software Engineering Institute (SEI) at Carnegie Mellon University, sponsored by the US Department of Defense. Its framework came from Watts Humphrey in the late 1980s, and version 1.1 was published in 1993. Each level from 2 up is defined by Key Process Areas (KPAs): the things an organisation must do well to reach that level.
LevelFocusKey process areas
1 InitialHeroicsNone
2 RepeatableProject managementRequirements management, software project planning, project tracking & oversight, subcontract management, software quality assurance (SQA), software configuration management (SCM)
3 DefinedEngineering processOrganisation process focus, organisation process definition, training programme, integrated software management, software product engineering, intergroup coordination, peer reviews
4 ManagedProduct and process qualityQuantitative process management, software quality management
5 OptimizingContinuous improvementDefect prevention, technology change management, process change management

CMMI (Capability Maturity Model Integration)

CMMI replaced the separate CMMs from about 2002. In its staged representation, two level names change:
LevelCMM (SW-CMM)CMMI
1InitialInitial
2RepeatableManaged
3DefinedDefined
4ManagedQuantitatively Managed
5OptimizingOptimizing
CMMI also has a continuous representation, which rates capability levels for individual process areas, alongside the staged maturity levels for the whole organisation.

CMM vs ISO 9001

ISO 9001 is a generic quality management system standard for any industry: you are certified or you are not. CMM is specific to software and graded on five levels, and it explicitly aims at continuous improvement. A company certified to ISO 9001 is roughly comparable to CMM Level 2–3.
🎯 IBPS exam alert "In CMMI, Level 2 is called?" → Managed. "In CMM, Level 2 is called?" → Repeatable. "Which level focuses on quantitative measurement?" → Level 4. "Defect prevention is a KPA of?" → Level 5. "SQA and SCM are KPAs of?" → Level 2.

7.8Solved Exam QuestionsADDED

Q1CMM was developed by: (a) IEEE (b) ISO (c) SEI, Carnegie Mellon University (d) ANSI
Answer: (c) SEI.
Q2At which CMM level is the process described as ad hoc and chaotic?
Answer: Level 1, Initial.
Q3An organisation that collects detailed process and product metrics and controls them statistically is at CMM level:
Answer: 4, Managed (CMMI: Quantitatively Managed).
Q4Which is a key process area of CMM Level 5? (a) Peer reviews (b) Requirements management (c) Defect prevention (d) Training programme
Answer: (c) Defect prevention. Peer reviews and training are Level 3; requirements management is Level 2.
Q5How many maturity levels does CMM have?
Answer: 5.

⚡ Rapid Revision

  • CMM = process-improvement framework (not an SDLC model), from SEI, Carnegie Mellon. Dhoni: focus on the process and results follow.
  • 1 Initial: chaotic, no documentation, heroics (new shop).
  • 2 Repeatable: basic project management, repeat past success (gym routine). KPAs: requirements management, planning, tracking, subcontract, SQA, SCM.
  • 3 Defined: organisation-wide standard documented process; risk management starts. KPAs: training, peer reviews, intergroup coordination…
  • 4 Managed: quantitative; predictable budget and time.
  • 5 Optimizing: continuous improvement, innovation, defect prevention.
  • CMMI names: Initial · Managed · Defined · Quantitatively Managed · Optimizing.
  • Levels can't be skipped. ISO 9001 = generic, pass/fail; CMM = software-specific, five levels.
