Chapter 5

Types of Networks

Chapter 4 classified networks by shape (topology). This chapter classifies them by size, the geographical area they cover. The lecture's one rule ties it all together: as the distance grows, the speed falls, and ownership moves from private to public.

5.0Classification by Area

🎓 From the lecture PAN, LAN, CAN, MAN, WAN: the "AN" is common and means "Area Network". The first letter tells you how big the area is: Personal, Local, Campus, Metropolitan, and the biggest, Wide. "Jaisa naam, vaisa kaam."

5.1PAN (Personal Area Network)

PAN: a network of devices around one person: phone, earbuds, smartwatch, laptop, wireless mouse.
🎓 From the lecture Effective range: about 100 m, limited to a home or a particular room. It provides extremely high speed. It is used privately: personal use, so obviously not public.
➕ Technologies Wireless PAN (WPAN): Bluetooth (IEEE 802.15.1), Zigbee (802.15.4, IoT), infrared (IrDA), NFC (≈ 4 cm, used for tap-to-pay cards and UPI Lite), UWB. Wired PAN: USB, Thunderbolt.
⚠️ Exam trap (Range) The lecture uses 100 m as the outer limit. Most textbooks and MCQs quote a PAN range of about 10 metres (typical Bluetooth range). In the exam, choose ~10 m unless the options suggest otherwise.

5.2LAN (Local Area Network)

LAN: a network covering a small area, such as one office, building, home or computer lab, usually owned by one organisation.
🎓 From the lecture Its reach grows to about 2 km. It is used in big offices, and we use it in colleges too. Speed is very high (slightly less than PAN's extremely high). It is generally private.
➕ Key facts
  • Technologies: Ethernet (IEEE 802.3) and Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11), which is a WLAN. Older: Token Ring (802.5).
  • Speeds: 10 Mbps → 100 Mbps (Fast Ethernet) → 1 Gbps (Gigabit) → 10 Gbps and above.
  • Topologies: star (switched, the most common), bus and ring (old).
  • Devices: switches, hubs, access points, NICs. Low error rate, low delay, low cost.
  • Example: a bank branch network connecting its teller PCs, passbook printers and servers.

5.3CAN (Campus Area Network)

CAN: a network connecting several LANs across a group of buildings in a limited area: a university, a large institute or a corporate campus.
🎓 From the lecture "It's not just one building, it's a collection of buildings": big campuses, universities, large organisations. The range grows to about 5 km. Speed is high but lower than LAN, because as distance grows, speed falls. It is generally private, since the organisation wants its work to stay within itself.
➕ Notes The buildings' LANs are joined by a high-speed fibre backbone through switches and routers. Examples: an IIT or university campus, Infosys Mysuru, a bank's head-office complex. Some books treat CAN as a large LAN, or as a type of MAN.

5.4MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)

MAN: a network spanning a city or a large town, connecting LANs and CANs across the city.
🎓 From the lecture Range: 5 to 50 km; think of a whole city, as with metropolitan cities. Speed must be compromised to an average level. Ownership: private or public. At such a scale things are generally public, but they can be kept private.
➕ Technologies and examples Cable-TV networks, a city's Wi-Fi / WiMAX (IEEE 802.16), DQDB (Distributed Queue Dual Bus, IEEE 802.6), the classic MAN standard, metro Ethernet and fibre rings. Examples: a city-wide network linking all branches of a bank in Mumbai, a city police network, smart-city networks.

5.5WAN (Wide Area Network)

WAN: a network spanning a very large area (states, countries, continents), connecting many LANs and MANs. The Internet is the largest WAN.
🎓 From the lecture It spans countries and states. Distance: more than 50 km, with no upper limit stated. Speed is lower because the distance is so large. It can be private or public.
➕ WAN technologies Leased lines (T1 = 1.544 Mbps, E1 = 2.048 Mbps), MPLS (most bank networks), Frame Relay and ATM (older), ISDN, DSL, VSAT / satellite (remote branches and ATMs), 4G/5G, and the Internet with VPN overlays. A WAN is often owned by a telecom provider and leased by organisations. It has a higher error rate and delay than a LAN, and routers are its key devices.

TypeMeaning

⚡ Rapid Revision — Chapter 5

  • AN = Area Network; the first letter gives the size. Rule: distance ↑ → speed ↓, cost ↑, private → public.
  • PAN: personal (room/home); lecture ≈ 100 m, textbook ≈ 10 m; extremely high speed; private. Bluetooth 802.15.
  • LAN: office or building, ≈ 2 km; very high speed; private. Ethernet 802.3, Wi-Fi 802.11; bank branch.
  • CAN: a collection of buildings (university), ≈ 5 km; high speed; private.
  • MAN: a city, 5–50 km; average speed; public or private. Cable TV, WiMAX 802.16, DQDB 802.6.
  • WAN: across states and countries, > 50 km; lower speed; public or private. Internet (the largest), MPLS, leased lines, VSAT.
  • Others: WLAN, SAN (storage), VPN (secure tunnel over the Internet), HAN.
